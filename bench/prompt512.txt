The history of computing is a story of successive abstractions. Early machines were programmed by rewiring plugboards; later, stored programs let the same hardware run different tasks. Assembly language gave symbolic names to instructions, and compilers translated higher-level languages into machine code. Operating systems multiplexed scarce hardware between users and programs, and networks connected machines into systems far larger than any single computer. Each layer hid detail from the one above it, which is what made the growth in complexity manageable. The history of computing is a story of successive abstractions.
